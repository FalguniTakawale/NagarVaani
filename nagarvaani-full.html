What do L1 – L5 mean?
How NagarVaani decides which problem gets attention first

Every complaint gets a priority score out of 100. It is built from layers (L1–L5) so that the most dangerous problems rise to the top — not the ones that simply collect the most votes. Votes are a gentle final nudge only.

L1
Immediate safety risk

Is someone likely to get hurt or sick soon? Open manholes, exposed wires, flooded homes, fallen trees. The biggest single boost: safety issues always rank in the top band (90–99), ordered by severity — votes add at most one point.

L2
Season amplifier

The same problem is worse at the wrong time — a blocked drain in monsoon is far more urgent than in winter. This multiplies the severity. The season is fixed when the complaint is first scored, so later votes never change it.

L3
Type of problem

A base weight per category (drainage, water supply, electricity, road, garbage, trees). Life-critical services weigh more than cosmetic ones.

L4
People affected

Denser areas affect more people, so the same fault scores higher in a crowded ward than in a sparse one.

L5
Cross-district pattern

If similar complaints show up across several areas or districts, it points to a systemic infrastructure gap, and gets a bonus.

Votes
Community votes (weakest)

Applied last as a small multiplier. It can never flip a severity-based ranking — a 3-vote flooded drain can beat a 100-vote pothole.

Score colours: 80+ critical (red) · 55–79 needs attention (amber) · below 55 routine (green). Open any complaint to see its own breakdown: “Why this scores N”.

About NagarVaani
Your Voice, Your City — नगरवाणी

NagarVaani is an independent civic-tech platform built to give every Indian citizen — in a city of millions or a village of fifty — a public, trackable way to report civic problems and be heard on the merit of the problem, not on how many people shout about it.

It is not a government product and has no official affiliation with any state or central body. It's built as a demonstration of what a transparent, AI-assisted complaint pipeline can look like — from a citizen's first report to an official's action, in the citizen's own language.

Built for a hackathon

NagarVaani was built as a hackathon project exploring how AI can make civic complaint systems fairer and more transparent. It uses AI models for classification, priority scoring and translation — Whisper-class speech-to-text for voice notes, and large language models for understanding complaints in multiple Indian languages.

Questions or feedback? Reach out through the sign-in page, or via the project's repository.

What happens next
  1. Checked & scored. AI reads it in any language, then scores urgency by safety, season, people affected and pattern — not by votes.
  2. Made visible. It appears in your ward's feed and on the map, and neighbours can add "same issue here".
  3. Sent to officials. The responsible office sees a short AI brief, ranked by priority.
  4. You're kept posted. Track it with your complaint ID. Signed-in users also get status updates, and can dispute a "resolved" that isn't fixed.
Tips for a good report
  • Name the road, lane or landmark.
  • Say how long it has been happening.
  • Mention if anyone could be hurt — it raises priority.
  • Add a photo, or use your location.
  • Keep it about the problem — political or communal posts are filtered out.
Your privacy
You can report without an account. Anonymous reports carry no name or email. Your text and photos go only to the AI and storage services needed to process them.
Report corruption
Corruption reports are moderated and forwarded to the relevant vigilance officer.
